# 0005: Separate the page into 3 background zones

- Status: Superseded by 0007
- Date: 2026-09-26
- Supersedes: 0004 (only where the accent colors go)

## Context
After the brand restyle, the owner said they could not tell what was the
intro, what was a guideline, and what was the sign-up step. Every part looked
the same: a centered title with text below, separated only by whitespace. The
colored title labels from 0004 (coral, pink, green, and violet blocks) and a
multicolor letter band also read as "heavy" and "messy". The owner asked for
options and picked one.

## Options considered
- **3 zones by background:** the intro on cream, the guidelines on a light panel
  with numbered topics, and "How to join" on a navy block. The page structure is
  visible at a glance while scrolling.
- **Numbered chapters:** everything stays on cream, with "Part 1 / Part 2" markers
  and rules between them. It's the calmest option, but the zones are only
  labelled, not seen.
- **Separate cards:** each topic in its own bordered card. Every topic is clearly
  a box, but many boxes make the page busier.

## Decision
3 zones by background. The zone titles use Agrandir Grand Heavy, and the
numbered topics inside the guidelines use Sunday. The colored title labels and
the letter band are removed. Accents remain only on the rims of the 4 pins and
the 3 step numbers.

## Consequences
The structure of intro, then rules, then action is readable without reading
any text, and the navy block makes the call to action unmistakable. **Given up:**
- Most of the brand color. The page is now mostly cream and navy, and less
  like the posters.
- The design-skill rule against alternating dark and light sections. The navy
  zone is exactly that, accepted because it marks the one action.
- Some clarity at the boundary between the cream intro and the light panel.
  The two backgrounds are close, so a hairline border does part of the work.

## Generalizes to
**Long single pages: show the structure with space and surfaces, not
decoration.** When readers cannot tell a page's parts apart, group them into a
few zones with distinct backgrounds, and give the final action the strongest
contrast. This does NOT hold for short pages with only 2 or 3 blocks, where
whitespace is enough, or when the zone colors fight a brand's own backgrounds.
