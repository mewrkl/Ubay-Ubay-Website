# 0010: An ongoing artist call with a monthly deadline, not a one-time Cookout call

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-01
- Source: owner's instruction (PRD §1 to §2 framing is amended by a note at its top)

## Context
The site was built as a one-time call for UP Cookout 2026, a yearly campus
event at UP Cebu. It had one deadline (October 25) and sold pins during
November 5 to 15. The owner changed direction: pins are now general merch sold
at the Ubay-Ubay stall and the FASO merch table, and the call stays open with
no end date. This was a direct instruction with little deliberation. It's
recorded because it reverses the PRD's premise.

## Options considered
- **Keep the one-time Cookout call:** a clear date creates urgency, and the
  page can lean on a well-known event to explain why artists should join.
- **Ongoing call, deadline on the last day of every month:** the page never
  goes stale. Artists can join any month. The team gets a steady rhythm: collect
  designs this month, make the bundles next month.

## Decision
Ongoing call with a monthly deadline. All Cookout names and dates are removed
from the pages and the sample card picture. Guideline 1 keeps the pin mix
(2 food, 1 new original on theme, 1 pop culture), adds a bundle name logo, says a FASO artist designs the backcard, and says 1 free
copy of the bundle goes to the artist. Uploads that arrive after a month's
deadline count for the next month instead of being refused.

## Consequences
The site can stay up for good, with nothing to update each month. **Given up:**
- Urgency. "The last day of every month" pushes people less than one fixed
  date, so artists can put it off forever.
- The event hook. "Your art at UP Cookout" was a concrete reason to join. The
  intro now has to sell the idea by itself.

## Generalizes to
**One-time campaign page vs evergreen intake page.** When the same request
repeats on a cycle, make the cycle the deadline ("end of every month") and
write copy with no dates that will go stale. Does NOT hold when the work is
tied to a single event, or when a hard date drives most of the sign-ups. In
those cases, a dated page you take down afterwards does better.
